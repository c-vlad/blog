# Crafting Messages That Resonate

_How to develop messaging and positioning that authentically connects with your audience using values, motivations, and language patterns revealed through affinity data_

---

## The Business Problem

Your brand team unveils the new campaign:

**Creative Director:** "We tested five different messages. Here's the winner."

**Winning message (based on click-through rate):** _"Achieve Your Goals Faster: The Smart Way to Get Things Done"_

The campaign launches with beautiful creative, premium media placements, and celebrity endorsements. Three months later:

**Results:**

- Click-through rate: 4.2% (excellent)
- Landing page conversion: 0.8% (terrible)
- Cost per acquisition: $127 (target was $45)
- Customer feedback: "Feels generic" / "Not for me" / "Doesn't get what I need"

**What went wrong?**

The message tested well for _attention_ (clicks) but failed at _resonance_ (conversion). People clicked because it sounded good, but bounced because it didn't speak to their actual motivations.

This pattern repeats across messaging challenges:

<div class="grid-2"> <div>

**Common messaging failures:**

- High engagement, low conversion
- Message works in testing, fails in market
- Customers say "yes" to concept, don't buy
- Generic positioning that could apply to anyone
- Campaigns that win awards but not customers

</div> <div>

**Why traditional message testing fails:**

- Focus groups optimize for articulate responses
- A/B tests measure clicks, not deeper resonance
- Surveys ask what people want to hear
- Copy borrows category conventions
- Messaging based on features, not motivations

</div> </div>

{{< callout "warning" >}} **The cost of misaligned messaging:**

**Direct costs:**

- Campaign spend on wrong message: $200K-$2M
- Media waste (right placement, wrong message)
- Creative production and revision cycles

**Indirect costs:**

- Brand confusion (saying wrong things loudly)
- Missed positioning opportunities
- Competitor gains while you iterate
- Team morale (built campaign nobody responds to)

**Example:** B2B SaaS company messaging "Increase productivity and efficiency"

- Generic category language
- A/B tested well (who doesn't want efficiency?)
- Conversion rate: 0.6%
- Post-analysis showed: Audience had 24.8x affinity for "deep work" content
- They valued _focus and craft_, not _efficiency and productivity_
- Revised message: "Create space for meaningful work"
- Conversion rate: 2.8% (367% improvement) {{< /callout >}}

**What's needed:** A framework for developing messages that authentically align with audience values, motivations, and identity - not just what sounds good in testing.

## The Data Approach

### The Core Principle

{{< callout "highlight" >}} **Resonant messaging speaks to who people are, not what they think they want.**

Effective messages emerge from understanding:

- What values shape their worldview
- What identity they claim
- What language they naturally use
- What motivations drive decisions
- What they reject (anti-values matter as much as values)

Affinity data reveals all of this through observed behavior, not stated preferences. {{< /callout >}}

### The Framework: Message Development from Affinity Patterns

**Step 1: Map core values through cross-category affinities**

Values aren't what people say matters. Values are revealed by what they actually engage with.

````mermaid
```

**Step 2: Identify language patterns**

How does your audience talk about what they care about?

| Source | What to Extract |
|--------|----------------|
| **High-affinity content** | Terminology, framing, metaphors |
| **Communities they join** | Insider language, shared references |
| **Creators they follow** | Tone, style, vocabulary |
| **Brands they love** | Messaging patterns, value articulation |

**Step 3: Map motivational hierarchy**

Not all values are equal. Some are core identity, others are secondary.
```
Motivation Hierarchy = (Affinity strength × Behavioral consistency × Rejection of alternatives)
````

**Step 4: Develop message architecture**

- **Core positioning:** Identity-level message (who are we FOR)
- **Value props:** Motivation-level messages (why choose us)
- **Feature messaging:** Functional benefits (what we do)
- **Proof points:** Evidence that validates claims

**Step 5: Validate against anti-patterns**

What do they reject? Messages must avoid anti-values as much as embrace values.

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. Optimizing for Clicks Instead of Resonance

{{< callout "warning" >}} **The trap:** "This headline got 4% CTR, so it must be good"

**Why it fails:** Attention ≠ resonance. People click generic promises, but only convert on authentic alignment.

**Example from MindfulTask:**

- "Get More Done, Faster": 3.2% CTR, 18% trial-to-paid
- "A Thinking Space for Your Life's Work": 4.8% CTR, 34% trial-to-paid

**Second message:**

- +50% more clicks AND
- +89% more conversions

**The gap:** First message gets attention through generic appeal. Second gets attention + resonance through value alignment.

**How to avoid:**

- Test conversion AND engagement, not just clicks
- Measure resonance metrics: time on page, scroll depth, trial-to-paid
- Interview converts: "What in our messaging made you choose us?" {{< /callout >}}

### 2. Borrowing Category Language Instead of Audience Language

{{< callout "warning" >}} **The trap:** "Competitors say 'boost productivity,' so we should too"

**Why it fails:** Category language is generic by definition. Differentiation requires speaking differently.

**Example from MindfulTask:**

- Category language: "productivity," "efficiency," "organize," "collaborate"
- Audience language: "deep work," "thinking space," "craft," "meaningful"

**Result of category language:**

- Sounds like everyone else
- Competes on features (which can be copied)
- No emotional connection

**Result of audience language:**

- Sounds unique and specific
- Competes on values (which can't be copied)
- Emotional resonance

**How to avoid:**

- List all competitor messaging
- Identify common patterns (that's category language)
- Deliberately use different framing
- Borrow language from high-affinity content creators, not competitors {{< /callout >}}

### 3. Ignoring Anti-Values in Messaging

{{< callout "warning" >}} **The trap:** "Let's use 'hustle' language because it's motivating"

**Why it fails:** If your audience actively rejects a value, using that language repels them

**Example from MindfulTask:**

- Hustle language test: "10x your productivity, crush your goals"
- Result: 0.6% conversion, 82% bounce rate
- Audience has 2.8x affinity for Gary Vee (actively avoids)

**Critical insight:** Anti-values matter as much as values

- Must avoid hustle language
- Must avoid efficiency framing
- Must avoid measurement/tracking emphasis

**How to avoid:**

- Check affinity for value opposites
- If affinity < 3x for popular movements, audience rejects them
- Explicitly position AGAINST anti-values: "Not another to-do list" {{< /callout >}}

### 4. Feature-First Instead of Values-First Messaging

{{< callout "warning" >}} **The trap:** "Our features are great, let's lead with them"

**Why it fails:** Features describe what you do. Values describe who it's for.

**Example from MindfulTask:**

**Feature-first (generic):**

- "Flexible workspace, note-taking, task management"
- Could describe 50 apps
- No emotional connection

**Values-first (specific):**

- "A thinking space for your life's work"
- Describes WHO it's for (thoughtful makers)
- WHY they'd care (meaningful work, not busy work)
- Features become evidence of values, not the message itself

**How to avoid:**

- Lead with identity/values
- Use features as proof points
- Structure: WHO you're for → WHY they'd care → WHAT you offer → HOW it works {{< /callout >}}

## Complementary Approaches

### When Affinity Data Isn't Enough

{{< expand "Message Mining from Customer Interviews" >}} **Method:**

- Interview high-value customers
- Ask: "How do you describe us to others?"
- Listen for exact language they use

**Example from MindfulTask:**

**Interviewer:** "How would you describe MindfulTask to a colleague?"

**Customer A:** "It's like... a thinking companion. Most apps want you to just check boxes. This helps me actually think through my work."

**Customer B:** "I tell people it's where my ideas live. I don't just track tasks—I build my knowledge there."

**Customer C:** "It's the opposite of those anxiety-inducing to-do lists. It feels calm and spacious."

**Extracted language:**

- "Thinking companion"
- "Where ideas live"
- "Calm and spacious"
- "Opposite of anxiety-inducing"

**Value:** Real language from real users, not marketing assumptions {{< /expand >}}

{{< expand "Competitor Message Differentiation Mapping" >}} **Method:**

- Audit all competitor messaging
- Map common themes (that's the category language to avoid)
- Find the gaps (that's your differentiation opportunity)

**Example from MindfulTask:**

**Category messaging (what everyone says):**

- Productivity
- Efficiency
- Organization
- Collaboration
- Goals

**MindfulTask differentiation (what nobody says):**

- Thinking
- Craft
- Meaningful
- Knowledge
- Depth

**Value:** Ensures you sound different from category noise {{< /expand >}}

{{< expand "Values-Based Customer Segmentation" >}} **Method:**

- Segment customers by which values they respond to
- Create messaging variants for each segment
- Personalize based on segment

**Example from MindfulTask:**

**Segment 1: Knowledge Cultivators** (Obsidian 34.2x, Zettelkasten 38.4x)

- Message: "Build your second brain"
- Emphasize: Connections, knowledge graphs, networked thought

**Segment 2: Craft-Focused Creators** (Austin Kleon 24.8x, slow productivity 31.2x)

- Message: "Space for your life's work"
- Emphasize: Process, depth, meaningful creation

**Segment 3: Independent Makers** (Indie Hackers 26.8x, building in public 24.2x)

- Message: "Works the way you work"
- Emphasize: Flexibility, autonomy, personal systems

**Value:** More precise messaging for each motivational profile {{< /expand >}}

## Actionable Takeaway

{{< callout "highlight" >}} **To craft messages that truly resonate:**

1. **Map core values through affinity patterns**
    - What do they engage with at 15x+ affinity?
    - What patterns emerge across categories?
    - What are the common motivational themes?
2. **Identify anti-values (what they reject)**
    - What has <5x affinity that you'd expect to be higher?
    - What popular movements do they avoid?
    - These are as important as values—actively avoid this language
3. **Extract language from high-affinity content**
    - How do their favorite creators talk about work/life/values?
    - What metaphors and framing do they use?
    - Borrow language patterns, not competitor messaging
4. **Build message hierarchy:**
    - **Identity level:** Who is this for? (thoughtful makers, not task completers)
    - **Values level:** Why would they care? (meaningful work, not productivity)
    - **Functional level:** What does it do? (features as proof of values)
5. **Test for resonance, not just attention:**
    - Measure conversion, not just clicks
    - Track retention and engagement
    - Interview customers about what messaging resonated

**Red flags your messaging is wrong:**

- Sounds like competitors (category language)
- High clicks, low conversion (attention without resonance)
- Generic promises ("boost productivity," "achieve goals")
- No emotional response from target audience
- Features-first instead of values-first
